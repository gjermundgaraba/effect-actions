# Limits

An authorizer fails only with a refusal; an operational failure a caller should see, such as a rate limit, is the handler's: its action declares the error in `error`, the builder yields the limiter, a startup service every surface of the layer graph shares, and the handler fails with the error before its work. Actions sharing a limit spread one error array into their `error` and wrap their handlers in one function. This reverses 83117ee's checks, the unreleased `Action.Check` that surfaces ran after authorization: no application declared one, and it cost a concept, four exports, an option, a type parameter on every action, a layer every surface required and an ordering rule, for one call and a declared error. Lost: the guarantee that every action listing a limit runs it; a handler that leaves the call out is unlimited, though one failing with an undeclared error does not compile. Rejected: a hook failing with the union of its actions' declared errors, which an action added without the limit received as a defect; a free-form `before` map beside authorization, two mechanisms for one job; and making authorization a limit, which would either let a surface's layer replace an application's permission rule or put that rule into every contract. A limit counting every request, decoded or not, is HTTP middleware ("Authentication descriptor and provider").
