# A scope per call

Each call has a scope of its own, opened by the shared dispatch around the authorizer and the handler, and `implement` leaves `Scope` out of the request map, so no surface handles it. A Toolkit call held what it acquired until its caller's scope closed, called directly or approved through `LanguageModel`: with a pooled connection per call, a third call on a pool of two waited forever, and `runStdio`, each tool and `generateText` required a `Scope` nothing used. Opened in dispatch rather than around tool calls alone, the scope covers HTTP too, where a call's finalizers run before the route middleware resumes and before the success is encoded; action codecs are service-free, so nothing after the call reads what it acquired. A resource that must outlive a call is the builder's, or a service the host provides around its calls.
